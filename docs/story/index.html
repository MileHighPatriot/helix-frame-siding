Story

A framing crew that stayed to close the walls.

Helix is not a general contractor that happens to own a nail gun. The company still self-performs the frame and the siding, and it hires the other trades the way a lead carpenter hires a specialist: on purpose, on a schedule.

  1. 2011

    A bay in Globeville

    Lena Hart and two framers rented a bay off Washington Street and took wall packages other crews did not want. The name Helix came from the way a stair opening twists through a floor: a simple shape that has to be exact.

  2. 2014

    Whole houses, not just walls

    The first full custom frame was a Wheat Ridge bungalow rebuild. Owners started asking Helix to stay through the roof instead of handing the shell to whoever was free.

  3. 2017

    The envelope crew

    After three jobs where the siding crew missed the flashing we had framed for, Helix hired an envelope lead and started closing the walls we had just stood up.

  4. 2020

    A bench of trades

    Remodels made it obvious that a framer who will not call the plumber is a delay. Helix formalized a trade bench: people we schedule, not a list we hand to the owner.

  5. 2024

    Decatur Street

    The shop moved to a bay in the Highlands, with an estimator and a coordinator on staff. Framing and siding stayed in house. Everything else still runs through partners who answer to our schedule.
